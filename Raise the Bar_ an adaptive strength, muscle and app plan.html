Your split, upgraded

Your instinct — one focus per day — is right. But your list leaves out two big muscle groups and trains everything only once a week.

Your version

ChestBicepsShouldersAbs and coreLegsBack?Triceps?
  • No back day. Your back is your biggest upper-body muscle group. It’s what makes you look wide and stand tall, which matters even more with a desk job.
  • No triceps. They make up most of your upper arm’s size. Big arms need both sides.
  • Each muscle only once a week. Twice a week makes it easier to get enough quality work in, and you get more practice on the big lifts, so strength climbs faster.
  • A whole day for biceps or abs. Small muscles recover quickly and fit at the end of other sessions.

The workouts

About 60 minutes each, warm-up included. The first exercise every day is the heavy strength lift; the rest build muscle. Each line is a slot: the app uses the first option your gym has working today that doesn’t clash with an injury, and the auto-enhancer sets its weight.

Warm up, 7–10 min

5 minutes of easy cardio, then arm circles, leg swings and hip openers. Do 2–3 lighter ramp-up sets of the first lift: roughly 50% × 8, 70% × 4, 85% × 2.

Work sets

Stop each set with the reps left the app asks for: 3 early in a block, 1 by the end. A rep only counts if it looks like your first one.

Rest properly

2–3 minutes after the heavy lift, about 2 after other big moves, 60–90 seconds after small ones. Rushing rest costs you reps.

Log every set

Weight, reps, and how many reps you had left. Three taps, and it’s all the auto-enhancer needs.

The auto-enhancer

It spends two weeks learning how strong you are on every exercise, then writes your weight and reps before each session. After your first set it checks whether today is a good or bad day, and after every session it updates what it knows about you.

  1. Log

    Weight, reps, and how many reps you had left.

  2. Estimate

    Every set becomes an estimated one-rep max, without ever testing one.

  3. Prescribe

    Picks the weight your gym actually has that lands you in the rep range at this week’s effort.

  4. Adjust

    After your first set, eases off or pushes on if today is very different from usual.

  5. Learn

    Updates your strength, your honesty correction and next week’s sets.

Weeks 1–2: calibration

Week 1

Find your weights

Every exercise starts light. Work up in sets of 8, noting reps left after each, and stop at the set where you had 2–3 left. That set is your starting point.

Week 2

First prescriptions

The app writes your sets at 3 reps left and compares what happens with what it predicted. Big misses get corrected straight away.

Weekly, from week 2

Honesty check

On the last set of one machine or cable exercise, you guess your reps left, then keep going until no clean rep is left. The gap becomes your personal correction. Most people have more in the tank than they think.

Honesty checks only ever happen on machines and cables, never on barbell lifts.

The maths, in one line

Estimated max = weight × (1 + (reps + reps left) ÷ 30)

This is the Epley formula, counting the reps you had left. 45 kg for 9 reps with 3 left estimates 63 kg. To prescribe, the app runs it backwards: for 6–8 reps with 3 left, 47.5 kg is the heaviest weight that fits, at 7 reps. Strength charts only use sets of 12 reps-to-failure or fewer, where the formula holds up best; for high-rep exercises the app only predicts within the same rep range, where its error mostly cancels out.

Rules it follows

Every session

  • Gains count in full. A better set raises your estimate straight away.
  • Bad days are noise. A slightly weaker session moves your estimate halfway down. One more than 8% below is ignored; two in a row and it counts.
  • The first-set check. If your first set lands 2 or more reps from the prediction, the rest of today’s sets move one weight step down or up.
  • One step at a time. If the next dumbbell is too big a jump, you stay and add reps first.

Every week

  • Sets per muscle adjust. Recovered and lifts going up: one more set next week, up to 20. Still sore or lifts dropping: one fewer. Joint pain in an exercise: it gets swapped.
  • Stalls get handled. A lift that hasn’t moved in 3 weeks is checked against your sleep, protein and skips, then gets a new variation or rep range next block.
  • Deloads can come early. Three or more stalled lifts at once brings the deload forward.
  • Sets reset after each deload and build up again through the next block.
Optional 10-second readiness checkBefore a session, tap how you slept and your energy, soreness and stress. Two bad answers make it a light day: one set fewer per exercise and one more rep left, same weights. Three or more, and the app offers the 25-minute minimum session. Short sleep mostly hurts big compound lifts, so the first-set check does most of the work.
When you outgrow the rackIf even the heaviest dumbbell or the bottom of the stack leaves you above the rep range, the app moves that exercise to a higher range, up to 20–30 reps, or a harder version: one arm at a time, a pause, or a slower lowering. Taken close to failure, lighter sets build about as much muscle as heavy ones, though heavy sets build more maximum strength.

Skips have a price

Every missed session is counted. One skip is free: the rotation just moves on, so no workout is lost. Skip twice within a week and make-up mode starts, adding extra work to your next few sessions. When it’s done, the plan goes back to normal. No money, no messages, no badges.

The timeout

You set a time

Pick a training time for each day, like 7:30 am before stand-up. A plan tied to a specific time and place is far more likely to happen.

It checks in

Two hours after that time with no workout started, it asks what’s going on: going later, minimum session, gym closed, sick, injured, or skipping.

Midnight decides

No workout and no reason by midnight, and the day times out as a skip. Reasons can’t be added the next day.

Make-up mode

It depends on how many unexcused skips you have in any 7 days.

1 skip

in 7 days

No penalty. The next workout moves to the next day, so nothing is lost. It’s still counted.

2 skips

in 7 days

Make-up mode for your next 4 sessions: a 15-minute block at the end of each one.

3 skips

in 7 days

Make-up mode for your next 6 sessions. That’s the cap: about a week of longer workouts.

4 or more

in 7 days

Still 6 sessions, and the Sunday review suggests a different training time or the 5-day version, because the plan isn’t fitting your week.

What’s in a make-up block

Two exercises, 2 sets each at your usual effort, for whichever muscles are furthest behind on their weekly sets. It never uses the muscles you trained that day, so the extra work gives back missed training instead of just wearing you out.

Example: you missed Tuesday and Wednesday, so Thursday is still Pull A. Its block adds leg press and leg curls, and the next session, Legs A, gets a chest and shoulder block.

Ground rules

  • Skip during make-up mode and the count goes up and the clock restarts, still capped at 6 sessions.
  • A minimum session still counts as done, but its make-up block still has to be done.
  • Clean slate. When the last block is done, the plan is back to normal.
  • Paused when you’re sick or injured, and picked up once you’re well.
  • After a week or more off, comeback mode replaces make-up mode. The first week back is for easing in, not catching up.
Never used as a penaltyNo skipped meals or cut calories, which would undo the muscle you’re building. No extra training days, no second workout in a day, no block longer than 15 minutes, and no muscle pushed past 20 sets a week.
Why it’s built like thisMissing a single day barely affects habit-building, but repeated misses do, so one skip is free and the penalty starts at two. Exercise used as punishment can make people dislike training, so the extra work is short, has an end date, and is real training for the muscles you missed. Sources are in the research section.
The 25-minute minimum sessionShort on time or energy? Do the day’s first lift and the next two exercises, 2 sets each. It counts as done, so “no time” is never a reason to skip. Use it more than twice in a week and the weekly review asks what’s going on.
Sick-day ruleSymptoms only above the neck, like a runny nose or a mild sore throat: train lighter or do the minimum session. Fever, chest congestion, a stomach bug, body aches or real fatigue: rest, and it doesn’t count. If you might be contagious, stay out of the shared office gym anyway.

Injury mode

An injury shouldn’t stop the whole plan. Injury mode takes out the movements that hurt, keeps everything else going, and rebuilds the injured area step by step, with pain as the guide.

Sore, or injured?

Normal training soreness

  • Dull and spread across the muscle, often on both sides
  • Starts 12–48 hours after training
  • Eases once you warm up

Train as normal, with a longer warm-up.

Possible injury

  • Sharp, pinching or inside a joint, usually on one side
  • Started during a set, or the morning after a heavy one
  • Gets worse as you move

Tap “Something hurts” and injury mode starts.

See a doctor or physio first ifyou heard or felt a pop, swelling or bruising appeared quickly, you have numbness, tingling or pain shooting down a limb, you can’t put weight on it or lift the limb, it hurts at night or at rest, or it hasn’t improved in two weeks. Chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness while training means stop now and get medical help. Injury mode keeps that area locked until you confirm you’ve been checked.

Rate pain from 0 to 10 during each set that uses the injured area, and again the next morning.

Green: carry on

Amber: hold

Same weight next time, no progression. Fine as long as it settles by the next morning.

Switch to the friendly option for today and step back one phase.

The next-morning rule: if it’s worse than your usual morning, step back one phase, whatever the light said during the set. This is the pain-monitoring approach physiotherapists use for tendon problems.

Four phases, moved by pain, not the calendar

Phase 1Calm it downUsually 1–3 days. Skip what provokes it, move the area gently without pain, and train everything else as normal.
Phase 2RebuildPain-free range only. About 40–60% of your old weight, 12–20 reps, slow lowering and holds.
Phase 3ReturnThe original exercise comes back at about 60% of your pre-injury weight, adding about 10% a session while the light stays green or amber.
Phase 4BackTwo sessions at your pre-injury weight with pain 0–2, and injury mode ends.

Complete rest isn’t the goal. Current sports-medicine guidance is to protect an injury briefly, then load it gradually, because long rest weakens tissue. The same guidance questions routine anti-inflammatories, so ask a doctor before taking them regularly. In injury mode, weights come from your saved pre-injury estimate, and the pain light replaces reps left as the signal to progress.

A sudden pop with bruising in the chest or the front of the upper arm can be a torn tendon. That needs a doctor quickly, not injury mode.

Hurt one side? Keep training the otherTraining the healthy limb helps the injured one hold on to its strength. In one study, people with an arm in a cast for four weeks kept nearly all its strength when they trained the other arm, while those who didn’t lost about a fifth. Injury mode switches to one-arm and one-leg versions for your healthy side. The evidence is strongest for arms.

This is general training guidance, not a diagnosis. If something isn’t clearly improving within a couple of weeks, see a physiotherapist.

Food: yes, it matters

Training is the signal; food and sleep are what actually build the muscle. Train hard but eat like before, and you’ll get a little stronger but grow very slowly. The good news: rough estimates are enough, because your weekly weigh-in corrects the errors. In the app, every target below is worked out from your setup screens.

1. Pick your goal

Lean bulk

If your waist, measured at the belly button, is less than half your height.

Eat a little above maintenance. Aim to gain about 0.5–1 kg a month; faster than that is mostly fat.

Recomp first

If your waist is more than half your height.

Eat around maintenance with high protein. Your weight stays about the same while your waist shrinks and your lifts go up.

Example: at 175 cm tall, half your height is 87.5 cm.

2. Your numbers

Protein a day

1.6–2 gper kg of body weight

70 kg means 112–140 g. Spread it over 4 meals of 25–40 g.

Calories a day, as a first guess

36 × kgfor a lean bulk; 31 × kg for a recomp

70 kg means about 2,500 kcal on a bulk or 2,200 on a recomp. Step 4 corrects the guess.

Carbs and fats

Don’t trackjust keep two habits

Keep your rotis and rice; they fuel training. Go easy on oil and ghee, which is where most hidden calories in Indian food live.

3. Track just two numbers

1

Protein, in grams

Log it as you eat, counting rotis, katoris and glasses. The portion guide below does the maths.

2

Morning weight

After the bathroom, before food or water. Judge only the 7-day average, never a single day.

Calories are optional. If protein is on target and your weekly average moves the right way, you’re eating right, even if every estimate was rough.

4. Adjust once a week

7-day average vs last week, on a lean bulkWhat to do
Up 0.1–0.25 kgPerfect. Change nothing.
Flat or down, two weeks runningAdd about 200 kcal a day, such as a glass of milk and a banana
Up 0.5 kg or more, after the first two weeksCut about 200 kcal a day, such as 2 fewer rotis or skipping the evening samosa or biscuits

On a recomp, aim for a flat weight and a waist that shrinks a little each month. Ignore the jump in week one: it’s mostly water and food, not fat.

5. Measure without measuring

  • Count in units you already use: roti, katori (a bowl of about 150 ml), glass (250 ml), egg, scoop, handful (about 30 g), and a palm-sized piece (about 100 g of cooked chicken or paneer).
  • Optional 10-minute calibration: weigh your usual roti, a katori of dal and your serving of rice once on a kitchen scale (about ₹300–500). After that you won’t need it.
  • Canteen and restaurant food carries more oil than home food. Assume 1–2 extra teaspoons per dish, about 50–100 kcal.
  • Being off by 20% is fine. The weekly weight trend catches it.

6. Logging it in the app

Three ways to log, fastest first. Whichever you use, the numbers come from an Indian food database, not from guesses.

Most meals

My usual meals

Most people eat the same dozen or so meals. Weigh each one once, save it, and from then on it’s a single tap. It’s also the most accurate method.

Anything else

Quick portions

Tap roti × 2, dal × 1 katori, curd × 1. The database turns those portions into protein and calories.

Eating out

Snap a photo

AI names the dishes and suggests portions, you fix them with + and −, and the database does the maths.

Let the AI identify the food, not count itIn a 2025 test of three leading AI models on 52 food photos, even the best were about a third off on calories on average, and all were more than 60% off on protein. A 2026 test of newer models found the same pattern: very consistent answers, but any single meal could be far off. They are good at naming foods, and better when told more about the dish. So in the app, the AI only suggests dishes and portions; you confirm them, and the numbers come from the database.

  1. Snap

    One photo of the plate, from above.

  2. Suggest

    The AI returns dish names and portions in rotis and katoris, as data, not a calorie count.

  3. Ask

    Two quick questions the photo can’t answer: canteen or home cooking, and any ghee or butter on top.

  4. Confirm

    Fix anything wrong with + and −. It takes about ten seconds.

  5. Calculate

    Protein and calories come from the database, with extra oil added for canteen food.

Which AI?

Gemini works for this, and so do vision models from OpenAI and Anthropic. Before building, photograph 15–20 of your usual meals, weigh them once, and see which model names them best for its price. Since it only has to name dishes and guess portions, a cheaper model may be enough.

More ideas you didn’t ask for

The first five came with the adaptive version. The rest carry over, because they still decide whether the plan works.

Book it like a meeting

Put each session in your work calendar at a fixed time. A specific when-and-where plan is one of the best-tested ways to actually follow through.

Fasting days

During Navratri, Ramzan or any other fast, use maintenance mode, train after you’ve eaten, and keep protein up with whatever your fast allows, like paneer, curd, milk or nuts.

A gym buddy

Train with a colleague when you can. Someone expecting you makes skipping harder, and starting a heavy set after a bad night’s sleep easier.

A little cardio

Two easy 20-minute sessions a week plus your steps look after your heart, and research says they don’t cost you muscle or strength. Do them after lifting or as a separate visit.

The Sunday review

Two minutes: sessions done, skips and penalties, lifts up or down, protein days hit and your weight trend. The app proposes changes, and you approve them.

Film one set a week

Your first lift, from the side. Compare it with a good tutorial, and ask the gym trainer to check it once a month.

Sleep is training too

7–9 hours with a steady bedtime. Short sleep mostly hits your big lifts, and on a bulk it means more fat gain.

Warm-ups and mobility

7–10 minutes before every session, already built in. Desk-stiff hips and upper back are where most gym injuries start.

Undo the desk

More pulling than pushing is built in. At work, stand up every hour and stretch your chest and hip flexors for two minutes.

A baseline check-up

A routine blood test before you start: CBC, vitamin D, B12, thyroid, lipids and blood sugar. Low vitamin D and B12 are common in India and quietly drain energy.

Creatine, if you want it

Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g a day, is the most researched supplement for strength and muscle. Skip it if you have kidney problems, and ask a doctor if you’re unsure.

Track more than the scale

Waist weekly, photos monthly in the same light and pose, and your lifts. Muscle comes slowly, and these show it first.

Abs are revealed, not built

Core work makes your abs stronger and thicker; lower body fat makes them visible. Crunches can’t burn belly fat.

The logic, in pseudocode

# ---------- Auto-enhancer ----------
def e1rm(weight, reps, reps_left):
    left = reps_left + profile.rir_bias              # learned honesty correction
    return weight * (1 + (reps + left) / 30)         # Epley, counting reps left

def predicted_reps(E, weight, target_left):
    return round(30 * (E / weight - 1) - target_left)

def next_weight(ex, target_left):                    # before every session
    w = ex.current_weight
    if w is None:                                    # first time: one rep above the bottom
        w = heaviest(x for x in gym.weights(ex)
                     if predicted_reps(ex.E, x, target_left) >= ex.lo + 1)
    elif predicted_reps(ex.E, w, target_left) > ex.hi:
        up = gym.step_up(ex, w)
        if predicted_reps(ex.E, up, target_left) >= ex.lo:
            w = up                                   # earned it, one step at a time
    while predicted_reps(ex.E, w, target_left) < ex.lo and gym.step_down(ex, w):
        w = gym.step_down(ex, w)
    return w

def after_first_set(ex, s):                          # adjusts the rest of today
    actual = s.reps + s.reps_left + profile.rir_bias
    gap = actual - predicted_reps(ex.E, s.weight, 0)
    if gap <= -2: return gym.step_down(ex, s.weight)
    if gap >= 2:  return gym.step_up(ex, s.weight)
    return s.weight

def after_session(ex, sets):
    S = max(e1rm(s.weight, s.reps, s.reps_left) for s in sets)
    if S >= ex.E:            ex.E, ex.bad_days = S, 0               # gains count in full
    elif S >= 0.92 * ex.E:   ex.E, ex.bad_days = (ex.E + S) / 2, 0  # small dip: halfway
    else:
        ex.bad_days += 1                                            # one bad day is noise
        if ex.bad_days >= 2: ex.E = (ex.E + S) / 2                  # two is a pattern

# ---------- Skips and make-up mode ----------
every night at 00:00:                                # the timeout
    if not trained(today) and not reason_given(today):
        skips.add(today)
        recent = skips.within_last(days=7)
        if len(recent) >= 2 and not comeback_mode:
            makeup.sessions_left = min(2 * len(recent), 6)   # restarts the clock
before each session:
    if makeup.sessions_left > 0 and not sick_or_injured:
        today.add_block(minutes=15, exercises=2, sets=2,
                        muscles=furthest_behind(exclude=today.muscles, weekly_cap=20))
when a make-up block is finished:
    makeup.sessions_left -= 1                        # 0 means back to normal

# ---------- Injuries and broken machines ----------
def pick_exercise(slot):
    for ex in slot.options:                          # ranked, best first
        if gym.working(ex) and not clashes(ex.stresses, profile.injuries):
            return ex
    return slot.home_version

# ---------- Plan changes ----------
def apply(change):                                   # from a button or the assistant
    draft = planner.rebuild(plan, change)            # template, slots, sets, time budget
    show_preview(changed_days(plan, draft),
                 weekly_sets(plan), weekly_sets(draft))
    if user_confirms():
        plan.previous, plan = plan, draft            # one-tap undo

Where your data lives

GitHub works as free storage, with one change to the plan: the token never goes inside the app, encrypted or not. To decrypt it, the app would need the decryption key too, so anyone with the app file could pull out both. Hardcoded keys in app files are the top risk on OWASP’s mobile list. Encrypt your data instead, and keep the token on a server.

On your phone

The real database

SQLite holds everything and works offline, so a workout never waits for the internet.

Public GitHub repo

The exercise library

JSON and photos, read with no token at all through GitHub’s raw file links. Updating the library is just a commit.

Private GitHub repo

Nightly backup

One encrypted file a day, written by the server function. It’s a backup, not a live database, because commits are slow and clash if two devices write at once.

Encrypt the backup, not the tokenBefore uploading, the phone encrypts the backup with AES-256-GCM, using a key derived from a passphrase only you know (with scrypt or Argon2id), so even GitHub can’t read your weight, injuries or food logs. Write the passphrase down: without it the backup can’t be opened, which is the point. Progress photos and your exercise clips stay on the phone and never go to GitHub.

One free server function

A Cloudflare Worker on the free plan holds every secret, so nothing sensitive ships inside the app. The free plan allows 100,000 requests a day, and you’ll use perhaps 20.

EndpointWhat it doesSecret it uses

  • Secrets live in the Worker’s encrypted settings, never in the app or in Git.
  • The phone proves it’s yours with a random device key kept in secure storage: Keychain on iPhone, Keystore on Android.
  • The GitHub token is fine-grained: one private repo, Contents read and write only, with an expiry date.
  • The Worker rate-limits and checks every input, and never logs photos or health data.
  • The 10 ms CPU limit isn’t a problem, because time spent waiting for the AI’s reply doesn’t count toward it.

The AI assistant: worth it, as a translator

Let the AI do this

  • Turn a message like “going home tomorrow till Sunday” into one structured change request.
  • Work out the dates, the equipment and the time you’ll have.
  • Ask one short question when something essential is missing.
  • Name the dishes in a food photo.

Keep the AI away from this

  • Writing workouts. It can invent exercises your gym doesn’t have, and give different answers to the same request.
  • Weights, sets and effort. The auto-enhancer already has your numbers.
  • Anything mid-workout. It needs the internet, and the basement may not have it.
  • Injury decisions. It only opens injury mode with the area filled in.

Once the buttons exist, it’s little extra work: one prompt, one output format, a checking step and the same preview screen. Any provider that can return JSON matching a schema will do, including Gemini, OpenAI and Anthropic models, and a small, cheap model is enough, because the job is reading one sentence, not planning. Build it in Phase 2, after the forms work without it.

What the research says

The main rules in this plan come from a study, a review or a clinical guideline. Where the evidence is thin, the plan says so.

Auto-enhancer

Setting weights by effort, using reps left, produced the same strength gains as fixed percentages of a tested max.

So the auto-enhancer isn’t magic. Its job is to find the right weights without max testing, and to react to good and bad days. Hickmott et al., Sports Medicine – Open, 2022

People usually stop with about one more rep in the tank than they think, and they judge better close to failure and in sets of 12 reps or fewer.

Hence the weekly honesty check and its correction. Halperin et al., Sports Medicine, 2022

One-rep-max formulas were most accurate from sets of fewer than 10 reps to failure.

Hence strength charts use low-to-moderate rep sets. Mayhew et al., JSCR, 2008

Light and heavy loads built similar muscle when sets went to failure, while heavy loads built more maximum strength.

Hence higher reps when you outgrow the rack, and light rebuilding sets in injury mode. Schoenfeld et al., JSCR, 2017

Inadequate sleep reduced maximal strength in compound lifts.

Hence the readiness check and the first-set adjustment. Knowles et al., Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport, 2018

Skips and make-up mode

Missing one day didn’t materially slow habit formation, but people who were very inconsistent never formed the habit.

Hence one skip is free, and make-up mode starts at two. Lally et al., European Journal of Social Psychology, 2010

If-then plans that fix when and where you’ll act had a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals across 94 tests.

Hence a fixed training time and the check-in. Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006

Physical-education bodies advise against exercise as punishment because it can make people dislike being active. That evidence comes from schools, not adults setting their own rules.

Hence make-up blocks are short, have an end date, and give back missed training instead of punishing drills. SHAPE America position statement

Doing cardio alongside lifting didn’t reduce muscle growth or maximum strength across 43 studies. Only explosive strength suffered a little, mostly when both were done in the same session.

Hence the cardio suggestion in More ideas. Schumann et al., Sports Medicine, 2022

Maximum strength was barely affected by up to four weeks without training, while stamina for higher reps dropped sooner.

Hence comeback mode trims sets before weight. Summary of Bosquet et al., 2013

Strength and muscle size held for up to 32 weeks on one session a week and one set per exercise, as long as the weights stayed heavy.

Hence maintenance mode for busy weeks. Spiering et al., JSCR, 2021

Exercise is usually fine with symptoms above the neck, but not with a fever, chest symptoms, an upset stomach, fatigue or widespread aches.

Hence the sick-day rule. Mayo Clinic

Plan changes

Across 25 studies, when weekly volume was equal, how many times a week a muscle was trained made no meaningful difference to how much it grew.

Hence switching from 6 days to 5, 4 or 3 keeps weekly sets first and frequency second. Schoenfeld et al., Journal of Sports Sciences, 2019

Injuries

Tendon patients who kept training with pain allowed up to about 5 out of 10, settling by the next morning, showed no harm compared with resting.

Hence the pain light and the next-morning rule. Silbernagel et al., American Journal of Sports Medicine, 2007

Current guidance for soft-tissue injuries: protect briefly, keep rest short, let pain decide when protection ends, then load progressively and stay active.

Hence the four phases, and training everything else. Dubois and Esculier, British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2020

Training the free arm while the other spent four weeks in a cast preserved the cast arm’s strength: a 2% loss, versus 22% without training.

Hence one-sided training for the healthy limb. Andrushko et al., Journal of Applied Physiology, 2018

Food logging

Three leading AI models estimating nutrition from 52 food photos: the best were about 36% off on calories on average, all were more than 60% off on protein, and errors grew with bigger portions.

Hence the AI suggests dishes and portions, and never supplies the numbers. Current Developments in Nutrition, 2025

Newer models gave very consistent answers, but any single meal could still be far off. The authors suggest naming the foods in the prompt and linking a food database.

Hence you confirm the dishes, and the database does the maths. Cureus poster, 2026

An AI model named the foods in meal photos correctly about 93% of the time and judged small portions well, but struggled with medium and large ones.

Hence portions are yours to set, in rotis and katoris. Nutrients, 2025

Telling the model more about the dish, such as its ingredients, made its estimates clearly better.

Hence the two quick questions after each photo. ChatGPT-5 food image study, 2025

The open Indian Nutrient Databank holds 1,095 raw foods and 1,014 common Indian recipes, built mainly from ICMR-NIN’s Indian Food Composition Tables 2017.

Hence the database behind every number. Vijayakumar et al., Current Developments in Nutrition, 2024

Some rules have no direct study behind them: the 8% bad-day threshold, the weekly set changes, the pain-light cut-offs for general gym injuries, and the make-up mode numbers. They’re sensible starting points, and the Sunday review is where you tune them.
